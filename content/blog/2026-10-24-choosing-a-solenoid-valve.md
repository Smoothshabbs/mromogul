Ordering the wrong solenoid valve is one of the most common reasons a pneumatic repair takes longer than it should. The part number usually tells you everything, once you know how to read it.

## 1. Ports and positions: 3/2, 5/2 and 5/3

The two numbers describe the valve: **ports / positions**.

| Valve | Ports | Positions | Typical use |
|---|---|---|---|
| **3/2** | 3 (supply, outlet, exhaust) | 2 | Single-acting (spring-return) cylinders, blow-off air, switching air on and off to a line |
| **5/2** | 5 (supply, two outlets, two exhausts) | 2 | Double-acting cylinders: one position extends, the other retracts |
| **5/3** | 5 | 3 (with a centre position) | Double-acting cylinders that must be able to stop mid-stroke or hold position |

If your cylinder has **one air port**, you need a 3/2 valve. If it has **two air ports** (most standard cylinders, such as the SC series), you need a 5/2 or 5/3 valve.

## 2. Single or double solenoid

- **Single solenoid (spring return):** the valve shifts while the coil is energised and returns when power is removed. If power fails, the cylinder goes back to its home position. This is the usual choice.
- **Double solenoid:** one coil at each end. The valve stays where it was last switched, even if power is lost. Use it when the cylinder must not move by itself after a power cut.

In the Techno 4V series the third digit of the part number shows this: **4V-210** is a 5/2 single solenoid, **4V-220** a 5/2 double solenoid and **4V-230** a 5/3 valve.

## 3. 5/3 centre positions

A 5/3 valve's centre position decides what happens when neither coil is energised: the cylinder can be **locked** (closed centre), **free to move** (exhaust centre) or **pressurised on both sides** (pressure centre). Check the symbol on the valve or its datasheet, and match it to what the machine needs.

## 4. Port size and flow

The size after the series number is the port thread: **M5, 1/8", 1/4", 3/8" or 1/2" BSP**. As a rough guide:

- **M5 to 1/8":** small cylinders (up to about 32 mm bore) and short strokes
- **1/4":** medium cylinders, around 40–63 mm bore
- **3/8" to 1/2":** large bores, long strokes or fast cycles

When replacing a valve, simply match the existing port size. For a new design, size the valve for the cylinder's air consumption, not just the thread.

## 5. Coil voltage and connector

Check the coil before ordering. **24 V DC** is standard on PLC-controlled machines, and **230 V AC** is common on simpler panels. The voltage is printed on the coil. A coil of the wrong voltage will either not operate the valve or burn out.

## 6. Don't forget the parts around the valve

- **Silencers** on the exhaust ports to reduce noise
- **Flow control valves** to set the cylinder speed (control the exhaust air for smooth motion)
- **Push-in fittings** of the right thread and tube size
- **An FRL unit** upstream. Clean, dry air is the biggest factor in valve life.
